The rules in one place

≈ 8 min · read

Read this part slowly once. You will not be tested on it. The exercises below will help you use it.

The big idea

Present Perfect

RESULT

have / has + V3

The action is done, and the result matters now. We care about what and how many.

We've submitted the dossier.

Present Perfect Continuous

PROCESS

have / has been + V-ing

We care about the activity itself: how long, how it is going, what we can see now.

We've been preparing the dossier since March.

Quick test: "How many?" points to the result. "How long?" points to the process.

Compare four tenses on one verb

The big question: is the action connected to now?

TenseExampleConnected to now?What it tells us
Past Simpledid / V2She cleaned the floors.No Finished, in a known past time.A fact. "When?": yesterday, at five, in 2019.
Past Continuouswas / were + V-ingShe was cleaning the floors when I came in.No It stays in the past. We don't know what happened after.An activity in progress at one moment in the past. Often the background, or interrupted.
Present Perfecthave / has + V3She has cleaned the floors.Yes The result is here now: the floors are clean.A finished result. "Is it done? How many?"
Present Perfect Continuoushave / has been + V-ingShe has been cleaning the floors.Yes The activity reaches now. The floor is wet, she is tired, maybe she is still cleaning.The activity itself. "How long? What has she been doing?"
Past Continuous or Present Perfect Continuous?

was cleaning = at that moment in the past. Then the story is over.
has been cleaning = from before until now. I can see the effect today.

In Regulatory Affairs

The reviewer was reading our response when the system crashed. (a past moment)
The reviewer has been reading our response since Monday. (up to now)

Talking to a friend

"How are you?" — I've been cleaning all morning, I'm exhausted! This links the activity to now. I was cleaning all morning, then I went shopping. This tells a finished story about the past. In everyday speech people often use either, but the first one answers the question "why am I like this now?".

How recent is "recent"?

There is no limit in days. What matters is the link with now. Present Perfect Continuous works in three cases:

  1. The action is still going. Any length: I've been working in Regulatory Affairs for ten years.
  2. It has just stopped, and you can see or feel the effect now. Minutes or hours, at most the same day: You look tired. — I've been reviewing labelling since six.
  3. Your time period is still open (this week, this month, this year, lately, recently): We've been getting a lot of questions from the authority this month.

If you name a finished time (yesterday, last week, two months ago, in 2023), use Past Simple or Past Continuous.

Exceptions and traps

A

Stative verbs have no continuous

know, be, have (own), like, want, need, believe, understand, belong

I've known the reviewer for years.I've been knowingI've been in Regulatory Affairs for 8 years.
B

live, work, study: both are fine

Almost no difference. The continuous sounds a bit more temporary or recent.

I've worked here for 5 years.I've been working here for 5 years.

"All my life" is only Present Perfect: I've lived in Dubai all my life.

C

A number means a result

With a count, use Present Perfect. Career summaries too.

I've submitted five variations.I've been submitting five variationsI've worked for three pharma companies.
D

Evidence you can see or feel

An activity left a trace? Continuous. A finished object? Perfect.

Your eyes are red. Have you been reading all night?Look! I've found the mistake.
E

Finished or not finished?

Perfect: it's done. Continuous: it may not be.

I've read the guideline. (done)I've been reading the guideline. (still going?)
F

Finished time word = Past Simple

yesterday, last week, in 2019, three days ago

We submitted the file last week.We have submitted the file last week

Repeated activity lately: I've been getting a lot of calls.

Same action, four tenses

≈ 5 min · write

Write one sentence in each tense about preparing a variation (or a renewal, or a response to the authority). Make the four sentences really different in meaning. Then compare with the model.

Past Simple: We prepared the variation last month.  Past Continuous: We were preparing the variation when the authority called.  Present Perfect: We've prepared the variation. It's ready to submit.  Present Perfect Continuous: We've been preparing the variation since March, and we're almost done.
